Tangible Deliverable

Before testing, agree on the thresholds, minimum sample sizes and review owners. Mark each check Pass, Hold or Not Applicable. Hold the decision when a required check fails or the evidence is incomplete.

Data quality and cleaning

Before accepting a cleaned training dataset

  1. Quality and labels

    Evidence to record
    Save a version of the raw data. Record missing values, invalid values and duplicates. Audit a sample of labels and record the disagreement rate.
    When to advance
    Critical issues are resolved and label disagreement meets the agreed limit.

  2. Representation

    Evidence to record
    Compare before-and-after counts and removal rates by group or source, where relevant.
    When to advance
    If a group exceeds the agreed removal limit, hold the decision until the reviewer resolves or accepts the difference and records why.

  3. Data leakage

    Evidence to record
    Count exact duplicates across training and test sets. Check for overlap at the required unit, such as customer ID.
    When to advance
    Confirm zero cross-split duplicates and zero overlap where entities must be independent. Fit learned preprocessing on training data only.

Bias and distribution shift

Before expanding a hiring-model pilot

  1. Local evidence

    Evidence to record
    Test on representative local cases. Record coverage by role or site and compare model performance with the current process using the same cases and independently reviewed, job-relevant labels.
    When to advance
    Local coverage and performance meet agreed criteria. Historical hiring decisions alone are insufficient labels.

  2. Missed candidates

    Evidence to record
    For each approved evaluation group, record qualified candidates missed ÷ all independently assessed qualified candidates. Include counts and uncertainty.
    When to advance
    Each rate and the largest gap meet the agreed limits. Hold where sample sizes are too small to judge.

  3. Pilot controls

    Evidence to record
    Record who reviews decisions, what is monitored and which results trigger a pause.
    When to advance
    Use a shadow pilot where predictions do not drive hiring decisions. Expand only after the review owner signs off.

Privacy and governance

Before advancing a federated learning design

  1. Data compatibility

    Evidence to record
    Compare schema, units, label definitions and coverage across participants. Record mismatches and how they will be resolved.
    When to advance
    Required fields and target definitions align across the participating sites.

  2. Update and model exposure

    Evidence to record
    Document who can access individual updates and the trained model. Test the proposed secure aggregation and access controls, confirm audit logging and assess compromised-participant and inference risks.
    When to advance
    The privacy and security reviewers accept the residual risk for the documented threat model.

  3. Privacy and utility

    Evidence to record
    If using differential privacy, record what unit is protected, the privacy budget and the assumptions used to calculate it. Compare utility with a baseline on the same test set.
    When to advance
    The privacy budget and any loss in utility meet limits agreed before testing.

Decision log

For each check, record the observed result, threshold, evidence link and owner. If a check is on hold, record the next action and review date. Explain any Not Applicable entry.

Record format
Check · Result and limit · Evidence link · Decision and owner · Next action and date

Worked example

Suppose a cleaning audit shows that 30 of 200 records from one source were removed (15%), above the team’s example limit of 10%. Mark the representation check Hold. Link the before-and-after counts, assign the data owner to review the exclusion rule, and set a date to rerun the check. The figures are illustrative.

Introduction

In the Workshop 4 Data Challenge Scenarios, I considered how data cleaning, distribution shift and privacy risks can affect an ML project. The workshop summary recommended turning those decisions into a checklist with measurable criteria. I adapted my reasoning into a worksheet an engineering team can use during a review.

Description

The worksheet covers a cleaned dataset, a hiring-model pilot and a federated learning design. Each scenario pairs the evidence to collect with conditions for advancing. The decision log and worked example show how to document results, name an owner and follow up when a check is on hold. These are proposed checks based on simulations.

Objective

Turn the risks identified in the coursework into checks that a reviewer can measure and document before approving the next step.

Process

  1. Preserved the three workshop scenarios and identified the decision each one asks a team to make.
  2. Translated the original reasoning into evidence requests. For example, the cleaning review compares removal rates, and the hiring review tracks qualified candidates the model misses.
  3. Used the workshop’s follow-up questions to distinguish update exposure from inference risks and added a way to record the remaining risks alongside model utility.

Tools and Technologies Used

I drew on the simulated workshop and its written summary. I used Word-compatible authoring and AI-assisted editing to format and refine the checklist. Google and NIST references support the evaluation and privacy concepts. Federated learning, secure aggregation and differential privacy are techniques evaluated in the scenario.

Value Proposition

Unique Value

The checklist makes each risk actionable. It specifies which measurement a reviewer should request, when the evidence is too weak to proceed and where to record the decision.

Relevance

Engineering leads can use it to structure an ML review and assign follow-up work. It demonstrates how I turn technical risks into decisions that teams can document, a skill I can use when helping organizations adopt reliable AI.
